# Final Result Analysis

## 1. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset**: NEU-DET (Northeastern University Steel Surface Defect Database).
- **Dataset Size**: Full benchmark on **1,800 images** across all 6 defect classes (300 images per class: 240 train, 60 validation).
- **Defect Classes**: `crazing`, `inclusion`, `patches`, `pitted_surface`, `rolled-in_scale`, `scratches`.
- **Evaluated Methods**:
  1. **PGRG** (Priority-Guided Region Growing — Phases 1 through 7)
  2. **Standard Region Growing** (Baseline 1 — FIFO queue, intensity similarity)
  3. **Otsu + Morphology** (Baseline 2 — Global Otsu binarization + elliptical opening/closing)
  4. **Watershed** (Baseline 3 — Marker-controlled morphological watershed)
- **Evaluation Protocol**: **Box-Level Localization Evaluation**. Connected components from predicted binary defect masks were converted to bounding rectangles and matched against PASCAL VOC ground-truth bounding boxes using greedy one-to-one matching with an **IoU threshold $\ge 0.5$**.
- **Timing Measurement**: Pure algorithm execution time measured in milliseconds using `time.perf_counter()`, strictly excluding image loading, preprocessing, bounding-box extraction, metric calculation, and disk I/O.

## 4. False Positive Analysis

A critical differentiator among the algorithms is the number of false-positive bounding boxes generated by background noise and surface texture artifacts:

- **PGRG FP**: **7,049**
- **Standard Region Growing FP**: **42,542**
- **Otsu + Morphology FP**: **37,671**
- **Watershed FP**: **10,116**

### False Positive Reduction by PGRG
- **vs. Standard Region Growing**: **$83.43\%$ reduction** in false-positive detections.
- **vs. Otsu + Morphology**: **$81.29\%$ reduction** in false-positive detections.
- **vs. Watershed**: **$30.32\%$ reduction** in false-positive detections.

Standard Region Growing and Otsu thresholding severely over-segment steel surface noise (e.g. grain boundaries and illumination gradients), generating over 37,000 to 42,000 spurious candidate boxes across 1,800 images. PGRG's combination of multi-feature priority weighting and Union-Find area filtering suppresses the vast majority of these false detections.

## 6. Objective Strengths of PGRG

1. **Highest Overall Localization F1 Score**: PGRG achieved an overall F1 of `0.0831`, outperforming Otsu + Morphology (`0.0228`) by $3.6\times$ and Standard Region Growing (`0.0199`) by $4.2\times$.
2. **Substantial False Positive Suppression**: PGRG reduced false positive box detections by **$83.43\%$** compared to Standard Region Growing (7,049 vs. 42,542) and by **$81.29\%$** compared to Otsu (7,049 vs. 37,671).
3. **Highest Precision Across Methods**: At `0.0646`, PGRG's precision is more than $3\times$ higher than Watershed (`0.0207`) and $5\times$ to $6\times$ higher than Otsu and Standard Region Growing.
4. **Highest True Positive Count and Recall**: PGRG successfully matched **487** ground-truth defect boxes (Recall: `0.1163`), higher than Otsu (483 TP), Standard RG (469 TP), and Watershed (214 TP).
5. **Class-Wise Dominance on Compact & Textured Defects**: PGRG attained the highest F1 score in 5 of the 6 classes, showing clear advantages on localized defects such as `inclusion` ($0.0971$ vs. $0.0010$–$0.0039$), `patches` ($0.1000$ vs. $0.0000$–$0.0028$), and `pitted_surface` ($0.1097$).

## 7. Objective Limitations of PGRG

1. **Higher Computational Runtime**: PGRG averaged **$70.86\text{ ms}$** per image. It is significantly slower than Otsu ($0.14\text{ ms}$) and Watershed ($0.98\text{ ms}$), and $43.91\%$ slower than Standard Region Growing ($49.24\text{ ms}$). This reflects the overhead of extracting multiple local features (Sobel gradient, intensity deviation, texture variance), constructing the continuous priority map, and maintaining a max-heap priority queue.
2. **Slightly Lower Matched Bounding-Box IoU**: When predictions successfully match ground truth ($\text{IoU} \ge 0.5$), PGRG's mean IoU is **`0.6789`**, which is $5.17\%$ lower than Standard Region Growing (`0.7159`) and $4.02\%$ lower than Otsu (`0.7073`). Because PGRG stops growth selectively based on priority and area filtering, its enclosing bounding box can be slightly tighter or looser than the broad ground-truth rectangles.
3. **Lower F1 on Elongated Defects (`scratches`)**: Watershed achieved a higher F1 score on scratches (`0.2524`) compared to PGRG (`0.1783`), as watershed ridges naturally trace narrow, high-contrast linear scratch fissures.
4. **Low Absolute Metrics on Diffuse Network Defects (`crazing`, `rolled-in_scale`)**: All evaluated methods scored low absolute F1 scores ($< 0.03$) on `crazing` and `rolled-in_scale`. Because crazing defects consist of complex networks of thin fissures distributed across entire steel sheets, single large ground-truth bounding boxes rarely overlap individual segmented crack fragments with $\text{IoU} \ge 0.5$.

## 8. Important Evaluation Limitation

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This evaluation is strictly a BOX-LEVEL LOCALIZATION EVALUATION, NOT a pixel-level segmentation evaluation.**
> 
> The NEU-DET dataset exclusively provides PASCAL VOC bounding-box annotations (`<bndbox>` with `xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax`). It does **not** contain ground-truth pixel-level segmentation masks, polygon vertices, or contour coordinates (`<segmented>0</segmented>` across all 1,800 XML files).
>
> Consequently:
> - Precision, Recall, and F1 score evaluate the ability of segmented connected-component bounding boxes to match ground-truth bounding boxes under the standard detection threshold ($\text{IoU} \ge 0.5$).
> - Mean IoU represents the bounding-box intersection over union of matched pairs, not pixel-level mask IoU or Dice coefficients.
> - These metrics evaluate defect detection and bounding-box localization accuracy; they must not be presented as exact boundary segmentation fidelity.

## 9. Final Research Conclusion

Based strictly on the measured benchmark results across the complete 1,800-image NEU-DET dataset:

1. **Did PGRG achieve the highest F1?**  
   **Yes.** PGRG achieved the highest overall F1 score (**`0.0831`**), substantially outperforming Watershed (`0.0295`), Otsu + Morphology (`0.0228`), and Standard Region Growing (`0.0199`).
2. **Did PGRG achieve the highest Precision?**  
   **Yes.** PGRG attained the highest precision (**`0.0646`**), driven by an $81\%\text{--}83\%$ reduction in false positive box detections compared to Otsu and Standard Region Growing.
3. **Did PGRG achieve the highest Recall?**  
   **Yes.** PGRG detected **487** true positive boxes, yielding the highest recall (**`0.1163`**), marginally higher than Otsu (`0.1153`) and Standard Region Growing (`0.1120`).
4. **Did PGRG achieve the highest Mean IoU?**  
   **No.** Standard Region Growing achieved the highest mean matched IoU (**`0.7159`**), followed by Otsu (`0.7073`), Watershed (`0.6894`), and PGRG (**`0.6789`**).
5. **Was PGRG the fastest?**  
   **No.** Otsu + Morphology was the fastest method by a large margin (**`0.14 ms`**), followed by Watershed (**`0.98 ms`**). PGRG was the slowest method (**`70.86 ms`**), due to feature extraction and heap-guided processing.
6. **Which defect classes were strongest for PGRG?**  
   PGRG performed best on localized and compact defects: **`scratches`** (F1 = `0.1783`), **`pitted_surface`** (F1 = `0.1097`), **`patches`** (F1 = `0.1000`), and **`inclusion`** (F1 = `0.0971`). It led in F1 across 5 of the 6 defect categories.
7. **What are the main limitations?**  
   The primary limitations of PGRG are higher computational latency ($70.86\text{ ms}$ per image), slightly lower matched bounding-box overlap tightness ($0.6789$ IoU), and limited localization performance on diffuse, full-image fissure networks (`crazing` and `rolled-in_scale`).
